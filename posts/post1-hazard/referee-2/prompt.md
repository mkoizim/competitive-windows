You are reviewing a 500-word research abstract before it is submitted to the SABR Analytics
Conference (general track). SABR evaluates submissions on originality, validity and applicability.
The abstract must be no more than 500 words including the title, and must contain a title, a review
of previous work, a summary of methodology, results, and the expected contribution.

The attached file, packet.md, is everything you get:
- (A) the abstract, verbatim;
- (B) context the abstract does not state;
- (C) the results behind it, generated from the study's results file.

Don't assume facts that aren't in the packet. If a point depends on outside knowledge, label it.
The author wrote the abstract and wants to keep it in his own voice: suggest targeted edits, not a
rewrite.

Write your report in this order:

1. **Restatement.** In three bullets, what the abstract claims. Note anything a first-time reader
   would find unclear.

2. **Claim-by-claim check.** For every number and claim in the abstract:
   - is it supported by section C;
   - is it stated accurately;
   - is any caveat missing that would change how a reader takes it?

   Quote the abstract's words and the supporting figure.

3. **Problems, ranked.** Label each Major (should be fixed before submission) or Minor (would
   improve it). For each, give:
   - the problem;
   - why it matters to a SABR reviewer;
   - a specific edit in the author's wording, with its effect on the word count.

   Keep the total under 500 words including the title.

4. **Omissions.** Section B lists results the author left out for focus. For each, say whether
   leaving it out misleads a reader, or is a reasonable choice for 500 words. Say the same for
   anything else in B or C you think a reviewer would expect to see.

5. **How a SABR reviewer would read it.** Reviewers are a mix of team and industry analysts and
   academics. In a few sentences each, say how they would judge originality, validity and
   applicability, and what, if anything, would lead them to reject it.

6. **Verdict.** Submit as is, submit with minor edits, or needs major revision. Name the single
   most important change.

Plain prose and bullets. No tables longer than ten rows.
